# OPINION: The Opioid Crisis Was a Business Plan, Not an Accident

**By [Your Name/Identity]**

I am not a statistic. I am a survivor.

For twenty years, I have watched friends die, families shatter, and my own life cycle through the revolving doors of addiction, prison, and rehab. For twenty years, I was told that this was my fault. I was told I had a "disease." I was told I made "bad choices."

But after looking at the receipts—the hard data buried in federal registers and financial reports—I realized something that changes everything:

**I am not a defect. I am a customer.**

The opioid crisis, the stimulant explosion, and the mass sedation of our foster children are not accidents. They are the predictable results of a machine designed to extract profit from human breakdown.

It sounds like a conspiracy theory. It isn’t. It’s much worse. It is a business model.

### The Architect: The Best Government Money Can Buy
In 1992, the government passed a law with a boring name: The Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA). It allowed pharmaceutical companies to pay the FDA directly to "fast-track" drug approvals.

Today, nearly **two-thirds** of the FDA’s budget for reviewing human drugs comes from the very industry it regulates. The "watchdog" is on the payroll. The result? Approvals got faster, safety checks got weaker, and the floodgates opened for drugs like OxyContin and generic Fentanyl.

The State didn't just allow this. They built the toll road.

### The Engine: The Silent Suppliers
While we watched the villains on TV, companies like Sandoz (a division of Novartis) flooded the market with generic opioids. They didn't need flashy ads; they just needed to fill the orders. When the lawsuits came, they paid millions in settlements for "misrepresenting risks"—a cost of doing business.

But they didn't act alone. Even our moral leaders took a cut. Investigative reports revealed that the Vatican’s own investment arm held millions in pharmaceutical stocks, including Novartis, while the pills were killing the faithful in the pews. It wasn't a secret meeting; it was just a diversified portfolio.

### The Enforcer: Economic Coercion
Why did doctors prescribe pills instead of physical therapy? Because the insurance industry made the pills cheap and the cure expensive.

A month of opioids costs a $10 copay. A month of physical therapy costs thousands. That isn’t a medical choice; it is **economic coercion**. The system funneled millions of working-class Americans into addiction because it was the cheapest way to manage their pain.

### The Trap: From Pain to Control
It didn't stop at pain. We are now watching the same playbook with stimulants. The DEA tripled the production quotas for amphetamines between 2006 and 2021. We are chemically binding our workforce to productivity, and when the prescription runs out, the new, cheaper, more toxic P2P meth is waiting on the street.

Even our children aren't safe. Foster youth are prescribed antipsychotic drugs at rates **3 to 5 times higher** than other children. We are using chemical straitjackets to manage trauma because pills are cheaper than love or therapy.

### The Verdict
The system defenders will say there was no "conspiracy." They will say it was just "misaligned incentives."

To that, I say: **If you build a car with no brakes to save money, you don't need to "plan" the crash. The crash is inevitable.**

We are the collateral damage of a system that decided chemical compliance was cheaper than human dignity. It is time we stopped accepting the shame for their profit.

The machine keeps turning because it is profitable. It will only stop when we stop seeing ourselves as "patients" and start seeing ourselves as **evidence**.

We survived. And now, we have the receipts.
